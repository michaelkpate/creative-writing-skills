### overall_story_pov

**narration_style:** [Choose appropriate POV and verb tense — common choices: Third Person Limited, First Person, Multiple POV; Past or Present Tense]
**story_type:** [Linear, dual timeline, etc.; note alternating POV or structural choices]

**pov_approach:**
- Balance character perspective and observable action.
- Ensure thoughts, feelings, and actions are shown clearly through the POV character(s) without excessive introspection or detachment.

**deep_pov:**
- Write as if inside the character's mind and body — the narrative should feel like their unfiltered experience.
- Avoid filter words (e.g., "she saw," "he felt," "I thought"). Present thoughts, feelings, and sensory perceptions directly.
- Interior monologue should sound like the character's own voice — their slang, biases, memories, and emotional filters. Never neutral or authorial.
- Describe emotions physically and viscerally. Use body language and sensory detail, not abstract labels ("she was sad"). Inner monologue conveying emotion must reference the character's unique voice.
- The external world is described through the lens of the character's immediate concerns, desires, and reactions. What they notice reveals their inner state.
- Limit authorial summary and exposition. Don't explain feelings or motivations — let the reader feel and interpret through the character's lived moment.

### baseline_prose_style

**clarity_and_conciseness:** [Favor clear, concise language that balances action, description, and internal experience.]
**description_approach:** [Use concrete, specific imagery to ground the reader — avoid overload and excessive metaphor use. Engage multiple senses only to sharpen the POV character's immediate state. Actions must be physically plausible — no confusing body contortions.]

**dialogue_style:**
- Dialogue should sound natural, unique to each character, and move the story forward — obliquely or outright.
- Favor action beats over dialogue tags to enhance scene description and deepen presence.
- Approved tags (use sparingly): said, asked, whispered, grumbled, muttered.
- Never echo another character's dialogue as a question — show incredulity or confusion another way.

**sentence_structure_variety:** [Rotate sentence length and starters for natural flow. Avoid repetitive patterns (e.g., runs of "I" or "He/She").]
**verb_choice:** [Prefer strong, active verbs over adverbs and filler. Passive voice only if intentional for tone or character experience.]
**pacing:** [One idea, action, or emotion per paragraph. Adjust white space for scene tension and dialogue.]
**diversify_vocabulary_and_avoid_predictable_phrasing:** [Use a broad range of accessible vocabulary. Match language to genre conventions and character voice. Keep it simple, active, and logical unless stylized voice demands otherwise.]
**engagement_priority:** [Prioritize clarity, coherence, and engagement — prose should be accessible, purposeful, and never bland.]
**stylized_language_control:** [Limit stylized language, metaphors, or genre-specific flourishes unless project or author style demands.]
**style_reference:** [Follow the Chicago Manual of Style for general punctuation, capitalization, and usage.]
**punctuation_preference:** [Use commas and full stops in place of em-dashes unless required for clarity. Do not use em-dashes except where absolutely necessary.]

**flaws_and_growth:** [Show strengths and weaknesses. Allow room for change and growth.]

### chapter_generation_instructions

- This is a genre-agnostic story intended to follow a clear narrative arc appropriate to its selected structure (e.g., 3-act, 4-act, 5-act).
- Every chapter must move the story forward through character decisions, conflict escalation, or new revelations.
- Balance dialogue, action, and introspection to match the emotional and thematic tone of the story.
- Begin each chapter with a hook or change in status quo to anchor the reader quickly.
- End chapters with a moment of transition, tension, reflection, or choice that compels the reader to continue.
- Maintain consistent POV and tense unless the worksheet explicitly allows otherwise.
- Incorporate any required subplots, tropes, or themes as specified in the outline or blueprint.
- Avoid filler — each scene should reveal character, build relationships, explore theme, or escalate stakes.
- When in doubt, prioritize emotional truth and forward momentum over exposition or internal monologue.
